# Short-notice train seats reward search, not speed

A personal tool can legitimately solve the search half of the short-notice problem, but not the booking half. The seat-finding tactics that work in 2026 (shifting the station pair on the same train, widening to nearby stations and all classes, catching current-booking seats after the first chart, and timing Tatkal correctly) are all read-only search and alerting problems, and those carry low legal risk. The booking step is different: IRCTC's terms state that "use of automation software and/or Scripting Software is strictly prohibited", Tatkal now needs an Aadhaar-authenticated account and a one-time password that only the account holder can enter, and IRCTC deactivated 3.04 crore accounts in 2025 through automated detection. Rule changes in 2025-2026 also shifted where seats appear. Waitlists are now capped, waitlisted passengers cannot board reserved coaches, charts are prepared 9-18 hours ahead instead of 4, boarding point can be changed until 30 minutes before departure, and cancelling inside 8 hours forfeits the whole fare. Together these make pair-shifting and post-chart current booking more valuable and make speculative or waitlist-based tactics worse. No official public API exists, so the tool has to rest on unofficial third-party data that must be re-checked on IRCTC before the user acts. The safe design is a private, single-account assistant that searches, ranks, times and alerts, then hands a prepared plan to the human, who logs in, solves the CAPTCHA, enters the OTP and pays. Two caveats on evidence: most rule statements here come from news coverage of Railway Board circulars rather than the circulars themselves, and the legal position of a private, human-paced autofill has never been tested in court.

## One day out, Sasaram to Delhi shows "Regret" almost everywhere

The running example shows how tight the problem is. A snapshot taken on 17 November 2026 for travel on 18 November showed **no class on any Sasaram-Delhi train with plain availability**. Most classes on most trains showed "Regret - No more booking", including every class on the daily Purushottam Express (12801) and 2A/3A/SL on the Netaji Express (13051). The remaining options were waitlists such as 3A WL90 on the Mahabodhi Express (12397) or 1A WL3 on a few trains ([ConfirmTkt](https://www.confirmtkt.com/trains/sasaram-to-new-delhi-train-tickets)). About 26 trains link Sasaram with the Delhi area, but only four run daily (12801, 12397, 13051 and special 04061), and they terminate at three different stations: New Delhi, Delhi Junction and Anand Vihar ([etrain.info](https://etrain.info/trains/Sasaram-SSM-to-New-Delhi-NDLS)). Against that backdrop, travellers work through seven families of tactics.

**Tatkal and Premium Tatkal.** Tatkal remains the main short-notice channel. It opens one day before the journey at 10:00 for AC classes and 11:00 for non-AC, with at most four passengers per booking ([EaseMyTrip, updated 12 Nov 2026](https://www.easemytrip.com/blog/tatkal-ticket-booking-guide)). Premium Tatkal opens at the same times on selected trains, with fares that rise as seats fill and confirmed tickets only (no waitlist), and agents cannot book it at all ([EaseMyTrip](https://www.easemytrip.com/blog/tatkal-ticket-booking-guide)). The traveller-controlled success factors are about preparation: passengers saved in IRCTC's Master List, a pre-loaded IRCTC eWallet (reported to complete payment in about two seconds without a bank OTP), and Vikalp ticked ([Business Today, 22 Jul 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/latest/trends/photo/2-new-irctc-features-and-3-secret-tricks-that-make-tatkal-booking-10x-faster-544575-2026-07-22)). One subtlety matters for a "within two days" trip. Sources disagree on whether "one day before" counts from the train's originating station ([erail](https://erail.in/indian-railway-rules-faq/tatkal-scheme)) or the boarding station (EaseMyTrip's wording). For trains that start their run a calendar day before reaching Sasaram, the Tatkal window could open a day earlier than a naive calculation suggests, so this needs checking on IRCTC per train.

**Shifting the station pair on the same train.** Berths are partitioned into quotas with separate waitlists: general (GNWL, from origin-side stations, best clearance odds), pooled (PQWL) and remote-location (RLWL) for intermediate stations, and a separate Tatkal pool ([HDFC Bank explainer](https://www.hdfc.bank.in/blogs/payzapp/what-does-gnwl--rlwl-mean-payzapp)). Booking from an upstream station (Gaya, Dhanbad, the train's origin) or to a station beyond Delhi moves the request into a different quota, at the cost of a longer fare. ixigo and ConfirmTkt already sell this as "Same Train Alternates", checking stations "before the source station or after the destination station" ([ixigo](https://www.ixigo.com/ixigo-same-train-alternates-get-confirmed-tickets-story-1174078)). A related variant is to split one train into two bookings (Sasaram to X, X to Delhi). The notes found no consumer app confirmed to offer this, and each ticket confirms independently. Which quota Sasaram falls under on each Delhi train could not be verified from open sources; it has to be read from live availability responses.

**Nearby stations and alternate trains.** The practical widening set is Pt. Deen Dayal Upadhyaya Junction (DDU), Gaya and Varanasi on the origin side, plus any Delhi terminal. Special trains (zero-prefixed numbers) showed the best confirmation chances in the snapshot, but they take 19-21 hours against 12-13 and charge higher fares ([ConfirmTkt](https://www.confirmtkt.com/trains/sasaram-to-new-delhi-train-tickets)). The list of premium trains reachable from DDU or Gaya was not verified in the notes and must come from live timetable data.

**Class-hopping.** In the same snapshot, 1A waitlists were tiny (WL3) with the highest chance figures (38-49%) even where 2A, 3A and Sleeper were closed ([ConfirmTkt](https://www.confirmtkt.com/trains/sasaram-to-new-delhi-train-tickets)). A search across every class is therefore worth more than one for the preferred class.

**Current booking after the chart.** Berths left vacant after the first chart are sold as "current booking" online and at counters until about 30 minutes before departure ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/travel/travel-news/2025/02/27/book-train-tickets-last-minute-charting.html)). A July 2026 report says some trains sell until 15 minutes before ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/indian-railways-lets-you-book-vande-bharat-seats-up-to-15-minutes-before-departure-now-heres-how-to-do-it-540209-2026-07-01)).

**Other official levers.** Vikalp lets a waitlisted passenger opt into up to five alternate trains at no extra fare ([Zee Business](https://zeebiz.com/indian-railways/news-confirmed-irctc-ticket-vikalp-increases-chance-to-get-confirmed-reservation-know-how-it-works-230655)). Emergency (HO) quota needs a genuine forwarding authority and a request lodged the previous day ([ThePrint/PTI](https://theprint.in/india/submit-emergency-quota-request-at-least-a-day-before-train-departure-railway-ministry/2699060)). Ladies, senior-citizen, Divyang and defence quotas appear as selectable options in availability engines ([etrain.info](https://etrain.info/trains/Sasaram-SSM-to-New-Delhi-NDLS)), but their current eligibility and sizes were not verified.

**Financial hedges and agents.** "Guarantee" products from ixigo and MakeMyTrip pay a multiple of the fare, partly as vouchers, if a ticket stays fully waitlisted at charting. They fund a fallback flight or bus and do not create a seat ([ixigo](https://www.ixigo.com/introducing-travel-guarantee-making-train-journeys-stress-free-story-1184884); [MakeMyTrip policy](https://promos.makemytrip.com/train-fc-policy.html)). Authorised agents can book for a fee but are locked out of the first 30 minutes of Tatkal. Touts who procure tickets as a business commit a criminal offence (see the legal section).

One old fallback is gone. Since 1 May 2025, waitlisted ticket holders cannot travel in Sleeper or AC coaches ([The Week](https://www.theweek.in/news/india/2025/05/01/indian-railway-5-rule-changes-may-1-waiting-list-tickets-not-allowed-sleeper-ac-coach-irctc-app-cancellation-fee-refund-rule-and-more.html)). Only a confirmed or RAC outcome is usable.

Four consequences follow for someone travelling in two days.

First, **the decisive moment has moved from four hours before departure to the previous evening**. The Ministry describes charting as now happening 9-18 hours ahead ([Trayaan, quoting PIB](https://www.trayaan.com/2026/03/indian-railways-announces-new-reforms-for-better-passenger-convenience-here-are-the-full-details.html)). Waitlist outcomes and unused quota berths therefore surface then, and the current-booking window that follows is much longer than it used to be. The best time to look is right after first-chart time. Whether current booking from an intermediate station such as Sasaram stays open until the train leaves that station, rather than its origin, is unverified.

Second, **"Regret" on one station pair no longer means the train is full for every pair**. Each quota has its own capped waitlist, so the cap makes pair-shifting more valuable than before. The boarding-point reform then makes the extended-origin ticket practical: book from an upstream station and formally set boarding to Sasaram. Secondary reports conflict on the cutoff (30 minutes versus "until second chart") and say it applies to confirmed or RAC tickets only ([GoodReturns](https://www.goodreturns.in/news/indian-railways-new-boarding-point-rules-irctc-2026-011-1501025.html)). Whether Tatkal tickets qualify was not found. Current-booking tickets cannot change boarding point ([Onmanorama](https://www.onmanorama.com/travel/travel-news/2025/02/27/book-train-tickets-last-minute-charting.html)), and failing to board at the recorded station lets the ticket examiner reallot the berth ([India Observers](https://indiaobservers.com/irctc-boarding-rule-confirmed-ticket-seat-cancel/)).

Third, **hedging by booking and cancelling is now expensive**. A confirmed ticket cancelled 8-24 hours out loses half its fare and inside 8 hours all of it, and a confirmed Tatkal ticket is never refundable. IRCTC's terms also prohibit "any speculative, false or fraudulent reservation" ([IRCTC Terms](https://contents.irctc.co.in/en/Terms%20and%20conditions.pdf)). Online rescheduling was announced in October 2025 but a guide updated in November 2026 says IRCTC still does not offer it ([LastBerth](https://lastberth.com/blog/how-to-change-journey-date-irctc-train-ticket-reschedule)).

Fourth, **an individual with a prepared account is structurally better placed at Tatkal than before**. Third-party apps book as agents and are shut out of the first 30 minutes, and the government says OTP verification was added "to curb misuse by automated bots and unauthorised agents" ([PIB Backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc202686945401.pdf)). The same OTP means unattended Tatkal booking is impossible by design.

The platform itself is also changing. A redesigned IRCTC site entered beta in July 2026, and trains began migrating to a new reservation system in August 2026 that targets 1.5 lakh bookings a minute against 32,000 today ([PIB Backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc202686945401.pdf)). Anything built against today's page structure will break.

## No official API exists, so the data layer is borrowed

Indian Railways publishes no public API for availability, fares or PNR status. Programmatic access is contract-only through IRCTC's B2B programme for ticketing agents, and the only fee figures found are dated and prohibitive: an integrator quoted a flat Rs 25 lakh per integration ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/buzz/irctc-revises-price-structure-for-otas-servicing-cost-may-increase-by-10-times)), and a 2016 agreement filed with the US SEC required Rs 50 lakh ([SEC filing](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1498232/000121465916014705/ex10_2.htm)). No current official fee schedule was found. RailOne, the unified app launched in July 2025, is a consumer app and not a developer interface ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/india-news/railways-launches-railone-app-unifying-passenger-services-125070101292_1.html)).

That leaves three kinds of borrowed data, each with different reliability.

| Layer | Source | What it gives | Limits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Timetable and stop sequences | RailRadar API | Schedules, trains between stations, live running | Free key allows 100 requests a month; no availability, fare or PNR; not an official feed ([RailRadar docs](https://railradar.in/docs)) |
| Static station and schedule data | DataMeet `railways` dataset | Station codes, per-stop schedules, CC0 licence | Undated and believed old; suitable for station lookup only ([datameet/railways](https://github.com/datameet/railways)) |
| Availability | RapidAPI `irctc1` host and similar | Seat availability by train, class, date; PNR status | Unofficial; pricing, rate limits and live-versus-cached behaviour could not be verified ([kapurshitij/irctc-mcp](https://github.com/kapurshitij/irctc-mcp)) |

The availability layer is the weak point. These providers are unofficial, and the research notes infer that they scrape consumer sites sitting behind IRCTC's anti-bot defences, so outages and silent staleness should be expected. Every figure is "as of a timestamp" and needs re-confirming on IRCTC before the user acts. Request budgets also force pruning: a train with N stops has N(N-1)/2 ordered station pairs, far too many to sweep blindly. No source lists which stations are quota boundaries per train, which is the input that would make pruning smart.

Existing software covers pieces of the problem but not the whole.

Licensed partners already do the core search. ixigo and ConfirmTkt run same-train alternates, and ConfirmTkt shows a confirmation chance beside each waitlist number. IRCTC's beta site and RailOne have their own predictor; the government states RailOne's accuracy improved from 53% to 94% ([PIB Backgrounder](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2026/aug/doc202686945401.pdf)), a claim with no independent validation. No third-party predictor publishes accuracy, and the 2025 waitlist cap and earlier charting have made pre-2025 heuristics stale.

IRCTC also has its own conversational assistant. AskDISHA 2.0 handles booking, cancellation and boarding-point changes by text or voice inside IRCTC's site and app ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/personal-finance/news/story/irctc-askdisha-2-ai-assistant-train-ticket-booking-refund-cancellation-478255-2025-05-29)).

Community projects show the feasible personal architecture. At least half a dozen small MCP servers expose train search, availability and PNR lookups to an LLM, all read-only and all on unofficial upstreams ([amith-vp/indian-railway-mcp](https://github.com/amith-vp/indian-railway-mcp)). None was found to sweep station pairs or send alerts, and none books.

Tatkal autofill extensions remain plentiful on the Chrome Web Store in 2026, several describing themselves as form-fill only with no CAPTCHA bypass ([IRCTC Tatkal Autofill Assistant listing](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hgiefnnhpkoikmpbdbehpacopdefjnag)). ixigo, an authorised partner, markets its own in-app Tatkal autofill ([ixigo](https://www.ixigo.com/trains/irctc-tatkal-autofill)). Older open-source Selenium bots are deprecated and predate the 2025 changes.

The gap a personal tool fills is therefore customisation: the user's own station set, all classes and quotas together, two-ticket splits, multi-leg routes through hubs, and persistent alerts tied to the rule calendar. Notification channels (Telegram, WhatsApp, SMS) were not researched.

## The law punishes ticket businesses; IRCTC's terms punish scripts

Two different regimes apply, and they draw their lines in different places.

**Criminal law targets the business of supplying tickets.** Section 143 of the Railways Act punishes anyone who, without being a railway servant or authorised agent, "carries on the business of procuring and supplying tickets", with up to three years' imprisonment, a fine up to Rs 10,000, or both; abetment carries the same punishment. In January 2025 the Supreme Court held that this covers e-tickets "irrespective of the mode of procurement and supply". The same judgment held that "Section 143 does not criminalise creating multiple user IDs" and that breaches of IRCTC's terms are for "civil action and not criminal action" ([Supreme Court, 2025 INSC 51](https://api.sci.gov.in/supremecourt/2017/6192/6192_2017_15_1501_58348_Judgement_09-Jan-2025.pdf)). The Madhya Pradesh High Court followed this in August 2026, quashing a case over two Tatkal tickets booked on a personal ID ([MP High Court order](https://www.livelaw.in/pdf_upload/2026/08/24/avinash-kumar-soni-v-railway-protection-force-695873.pdf)). That case took about six years from raid to quashing, which shows that being legally right is not the same as being left alone.

On the statute's wording, a person booking only their own or family travel is not carrying on a business. That is an inference from the text. No court has ruled squarely on a non-commercial individual using automation.

**Enforcement has followed distribution, circumvention and fake identities.** Every reported case in the notes involved software sold or rented to others, a defeated security control, or many fake IDs. The closest analogue to a personal assistant is the Super Tatkal app. Its developer was arrested in 2020 under the abetment clause for a paid speed-up app that passengers used on their own accounts, and Southern Railway said he had earned about Rs 20 lakh ([The News Minute](https://thenewsminute.com/article/ready-help-irctc-improve-booking-system-says-tn-techie-arrested-making-app-138513)). IRCTC's statement on that case went further than the statute: "Use of an application to fraudulently help few to jump the queue is illegal too" ([IRCTC press release, 2 Nov 2020](https://www.irctc.com/assets/images/pressrelease/IIT_Press_release.pdf)). The Real Mango case (about 50 arrests) centred on CAPTCHA bypass ([ETV Bharat](https://etvbharat.com/english/bharat/bharat-news/rpf-decimates-illegal-software-real-mango-arrests-50-touts/na20200908201454798)). No reported prosecution of an ordinary passenger for using a tool on their own account was found, though local cases are thinly reported and trial outcomes, including the Super Tatkal developer's, are unknown.

**IRCTC's contract draws no line between autofill and a bot.** The terms limit the site to "personal and non-commercial use", allow one registration per person, and say a user who "indulges in suspicious booking activities including usage of scripting tools, or Tatkal Softwares" can be deactivated and have tickets released "without providing any reason", with no refund ([IRCTC Terms](https://contents.irctc.co.in/en/Terms%20and%20conditions.pdf)). On a literal reading, a human-paced autofill script and a headless bot both breach the contract. They differ in detectability and criminal exposure, not in what the wording permits.

The realistic sanction is automated and hard to reverse. In 2025, 3.04 crore accounts were deactivated and 2.94 crore suspended, while only 1,80,474 were reactivated through February 2026. Railways also lodged 408 cybercrime complaints covering 4.07 lakh suspicious PNRs ([Ministry of Railways / PIB, 25 Mar 2026](https://pubt.io/view/F11A47142392AE0C6666DD1E93C2A558EEA1ABFC)). Detection thresholds are not published. Blogs attribute suspensions to extensions that fill forms in fractions of a second, but no verified instance was found ([LastBerth](https://lastberth.com/blog/how-to-unblock-irctc-id-deactivated-account)). Losing the account at Tatkal time means no ticket and possibly no refund.

**Read-only data use sits in the lowest-risk tier.** India has no scraping-specific statute and no reported case on scraping rail availability data. The exposure is contractual, plus a contested government view that scraping can amount to unauthorised access under Section 43 of the IT Act ([law.asia](https://law.asia/india-data-scraping-regulation/)). Risk rises when a tool polls IRCTC while logged in, and rises sharply if it evades anti-bot controls.

**Data protection is a minor constraint for a private tool.** The Digital Personal Data Protection Act does not apply to data an individual processes for a personal or domestic purpose ([SCC Online](https://www.scconline.com/blog/post/2025/12/26/digital-personal-data-protection-rules-2025-key-highlights/)), and its main obligations are reported to start on 13 May 2027 ([Mondaq](https://www.mondaq.com/india/privacy-protection/1708314/update-indias-digital-personal-data-protection-framework-comes-into-effect)). That exclusion is lost once the tool is offered to others. Credential security is the live concern: Aadhaar-verified IRCTC accounts have been hijacked and sold on Telegram bundled with autofill tools ([The Quint](https://www.thequint.com/news/aadhaar-linked-irctc-accounts-hacked-and-sold-on-telegram)).

The table summarises the research notes' synthesis. It is an inference from the sources above, not adjudicated law and not legal advice.

| Tier | Criminal exposure | IRCTC terms | Enforcement seen |
|---|---|---|---|
| Read-only search and alerts | Low; no ticket is procured or supplied | Breach only if the tool scripts IRCTC itself | None found against individuals; anti-bot blocking |
| Human-in-the-loop autofill, own account, own or family travel | Low on the statute's wording; untested | Literal breach | Deactivation and lost fare, reported by blogs only |
| Same tool sold, shared, or used to book for others for a fee | High; abetment for the developer | Breach | Super Tatkal arrest; arrests of software sellers |
| Unattended booking, CAPTCHA or OTP bypass, multiple or fake IDs | High; Railways Act and IT Act | Clear breach | Raids, arrests, mass deactivation |

## Design implications: an assistant that plans, with the human booking

The evidence sorts candidate features into three groups.

### Feasible and safe: search, ranking, timing and alerts

These features never touch the user's IRCTC session, so they engage neither Section 143 nor the automation clause.

A **station-pair sweep** is the highest-value feature. For each train through the user's station, it queries upstream-origin and downstream-destination variants, all classes, and the relevant quotas, then ranks results by availability, extra fare and journey time. It should read the waitlist type from each live response rather than hard-code quota boundaries, because those boundaries are not published. It should also state the conditions on each option: the longer fare, the need to set boarding point formally, and the open question of whether Tatkal tickets allow a boarding change.

A **station-group and multi-leg search** widens origin to DDU, Gaya and Varanasi and treats Delhi as a group of terminals. Multi-leg options need a clear warning that each leg is a separate booking; the notes found no through-booking scheme and treat missed-connection protection as absent, though unverified.

A **rule calendar** computes, per train and date, when Tatkal opens, when the first chart is due, when the emergency-quota deadline falls, and which refund slab applies at any moment. The Tatkal date must be computed and then verified per train because of the originating-station ambiguity.

**Alerts** fire at the moments seats appear: shortly before the Tatkal window, right after first-chart time for current booking, and when a pair flips from "Regret" to bookable. Polling should be slow (minutes, not seconds) and go through third-party data rather than a logged-in IRCTC session.

A **preparation checklist** covers what makes manual Tatkal fast: Aadhaar authentication confirmed, Master List within the six-passenger limit, eWallet funded, Vikalp choice decided, and the phone that receives the Aadhaar OTP in hand.

All of this is honest only if every availability figure carries its timestamp and source, and the tool says plainly that IRCTC is the final authority.

### Must stay human-in-the-loop: everything inside the IRCTC session

Login, CAPTCHA, Aadhaar OTP, bank OTP and payment belong to the account holder every time. The OTP requirement makes this a technical fact as well as a legal one. The decision to buy also stays with the human, because a confirmed Tatkal ticket is non-refundable and other tickets lose 50-100% inside 24 hours.

"Filling the Tatkal form faster" is the request that the evidence supports least. The sanctioned ways to be fast are IRCTC's own Master List, the browser's native autofill or password manager, and IRCTC's eWallet. A custom script that fills the passenger form is a literal terms breach even at human pace, and the cost of a false positive is the account itself at the worst possible moment. The marginal gain over a saved Master List is a few seconds of typing. If a user chooses it anyway, the notes' risk-reduction advice is human-speed pacing, passenger and journey fields only, and a manual fallback ready. That lowers risk; it does not create permission. The stronger design choice is a hand-off: the tool presents the exact train, class, quota, station pair and boarding point, and the user enters them in the official interface.

### Should not be built

Some features sit where arrests and mass deactivations have actually occurred.

Unattended or headless booking, CAPTCHA solving of any kind, and automatic reading and submission of OTPs defeat controls that IRCTC describes as existing "to prevent use of any illegal software".

Multiple, borrowed, rented or purchased IRCTC accounts breach the one-registration rule and are the usual evidence of a ticketing business.

Evasion of anti-bot systems (stealth browsers, fingerprint spoofing, proxy rotation, reverse-engineered IRCTC calls) strengthens an unauthorised-access argument. If IRCTC blocks the tool, the tool should stop.

Booking for others for any fee, or distributing, selling or advertising the tool as a booking accelerator, is what turned an autofill-style app into an abetment arrest. The tool should stay private.

Speculative hold-and-cancel strategies are prohibited by the terms and are now costly under the 72/24/8-hour slabs.

Recommending travel on a waitlisted ticket in a reserved coach would be recommending something barred since May 2025.

The tool should also avoid storing the IRCTC password, card details, OTPs or Aadhaar numbers, and should send no passenger data to unofficial APIs.

## Conclusion

The 2025-2026 reforms changed what a helper is useful for. By tying Tatkal to one Aadhaar-verified human and an OTP, and by filtering scripted traffic at scale, Railways removed most of the advantage that speed automation used to buy and raised its cost to the loss of the account. The same reforms (capped per-quota waitlists, charts the evening before, boarding changes until 30 minutes before departure) created a larger and more time-structured search space that a person cannot scan by hand under pressure. The contested ground has moved from the booking form to the plan: knowing which station pair, class and moment to try.

Three open questions decide how far such a tool can go, and none can be settled from published sources. The first is whether an affordable availability source is live and accurate enough near Tatkal and charting times. The second is how IRCTC would treat a private, human-paced autofill, where its 2020 statement and the Supreme Court's narrow reading of Section 143 point in different directions. The third is the set of unverified rule details that the best tactics depend on: the Tatkal date basis for trains that start a day earlier, boarding-point changes on Tatkal tickets, and current-booking cutoffs at intermediate stations. These should be checked against IRCTC and Railway Board circulars directly, since the rules changed several times in 2025-2026 and the booking platform itself is mid-migration.
